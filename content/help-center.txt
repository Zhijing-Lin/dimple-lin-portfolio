Needs-Driven Help Center for Tutor Performance Support
Using needs analysis, cross-functional collaboration, SME knowledge elicitation, and AI-assisted content development to turn recurring live-session challenges into actionable support.

Project Overview
My client, PLUS Tutoring, provides live tutoring programs that connect students with tutors for real-time academic support. During live sessions, tutors frequently encounter technical, operational, and student-related challenges and often rely on supervisors for recurring questions, creating repeated support requests and inconsistent access to guidance.

I designed a needs-driven Help Center and just-in-time performance support resource organized around the tasks and problems tutors encounter across the tutoring workflow. Some resources support pre-session preparation, while others provide actionable guidance during live sessions so tutors can resolve common issues more independently.

Reframing the Problem
From a Top-Down Help Center to a Needs-Driven Support System

Based on my manager’s initial brief, I designed the first Help Center information architecture around the core tools tutors used, including PLUS, Zoom, and Slack, and built an interactive prototype in Google AI Studio. I shared it with PLUS supervisors and a product designer, then completed two rounds of iteration based on their feedback.

While the structure became clearer and easier to navigate, I realized I was still designing from the content outward, organizing what we thought tutors might need rather than fully understanding the problems they were actually encountering.

After discussing this concern with my manager, I proposed shifting the project from a top-down content-design approach toward a bottom-up needs analysis.



Help Center Information Architecture Prototypes

Needs Analysis: Identifying Where Tutors Actually Get Stuck
To better understand tutors’ support needs, I used multiple sources to identify recurring problems and understand them in context.

1. Supervisor and Lead Tutor Interviews
I spoke with the people closest to tutor operations, supervisors and lead tutors, focusing on recurring breakdowns in live tutoring sessions.

2. Slack Analysis
To validate the interview findings against tutors’ real support behavior, I partnered with the Research Programmer to analyze the most frequently asked tutor questions in Slack, PLUS’s primary internal communication channel.

3. Experiencing the Workflow Firsthand
I participated in a live PLUS tutoring session as a tutor to experience the workflow firsthand. During the session, I documented moments where I felt confused, uncertain about the next step, or unable to quickly find the information I needed.

Intervention Selection
From Tutor Problems to the Right Intervention

After collecting recurring tutor issues, I organized them in Figma to make patterns across the problem space visible, grouping issues by both operational topic and underlying issue type. I then met with the PLUS Product Lead to review the problem space and determine the most appropriate response for each issue: Help Center content, a product feature, or a combination of both.

Immediate guidance needed + procedure exists → Help Center

Workflow is constrained by the platform → Product feature

Urgent current problem + longer-term platform opportunity → Help Center now + Product later

Performance Support Design & Develop
Matching the Support Format to the Type of Problem

After identifying which issues belonged in the Help Center, I designed two types of performance support based on the nature of the problem: predictable procedures and context-sensitive decisions.


Procedural Job Aid Example: Join & Participate in Sessions

A. Procedural Job Aids

For predictable workflows in the PLUS application, Zoom, and Slack, tutors mainly needed to know how to complete a specific task correctly.

I designed these resources to be: Short → Visual → Step-by-Step → Action First

B. Operational Decision Guides

Operational challenges, such as a tutor joining late, insufficient tutor coverage, or a language mismatch, required more context-sensitive judgment. In many cases, the correct response existed as tacit knowledge among experienced supervisors rather than as a clearly documented procedure.

I used weekly supervisor meetings as SME knowledge-elicitation sessions, bringing one or two priority problems to each meeting and asking follow-up questions to uncover the underlying decision rules, exceptions, and escalation points.

I then translated this expert knowledge into structured decision guides that helped tutors understand:

What to do

What to say through example language or messages

Practical tips for handling the situation

When to escalate the issue


Operational Decision Guide Example: When the Classroom Teacher Is Unavailable

Human-in-the-Loop AI Workflow

I used a human-in-the-loop AI workflow to develop individual Help Center articles. During weekly supervisor meetings, I used an AI note taker to capture the discussion, including procedures, decision points, and edge cases. After each meeting, I provided ChatGPT with the article topic, meeting transcript or notes, examples of previously completed articles, and the desired structure and tone to generate a first draft.

I then reviewed, rewrote, and re-prompted the draft to improve its accuracy, clarity, and consistency. Once the article was refined, I shared it with supervisors asynchronously for feedback and iterated again based on their comments before finalizing the content.
